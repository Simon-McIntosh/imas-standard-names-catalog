    vertical_coordinate_of_launching_position¶

    Vertical (Z) coordinate of the electron cyclotron wave launching position, specifying the height of the launcher above or below the machine midplane in the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame.

    The vertical coordinate \(Z_{\rm launch}\) locates the point at which an electron cyclotron (EC) beam enters the plasma in the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame, where \(Z\) measures height relative to the machine midplane, \(R\) is the major radius from the symmetry axis, and \(\phi\) is the toroidal angle. Together with major_radius_of_launching_position, it fully specifies the poloidal-plane position of the EC launcher aperture, which determines the initial ray trajectory used in beam-tracing or ray-tracing codes to model wave propagation and absorption in the plasma. Typical values range from approximately \(-2\) m to \(+2\) m for medium-to-large tokamaks, depending on launcher installation height and whether the beam is aimed from the low-field or high-field side. The launching position is usually defined at the antenna mirror or waveguide mouth and is provided as a fixed geometric parameter in the launcher design documentation or inferred from engineering surveys; it pairs with the mirror orientation angles described by toroidal_angle_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror to fully characterize the beam injection geometry.

    Sign convention: Positive when the launching position lies above the machine midplane in the \(+Z\) direction.

    Unit: m

    Kind: scalar

    vertical_coordinate_of_measurement_position¶

    Vertical Z coordinate locating a generic diagnostic or reflectometer measurement position in the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame, where \(Z\) is the height above or below the machine midplane.

    The vertical coordinate \(Z\) specifies the height of a measurement position in the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame, where \(R\) is the major radius from the machine symmetry axis, \(\phi\) is the toroidal angle increasing counter-clockwise when viewed from above, and \(Z\) is the vertical height in meters. This generic locus token applies across multiple diagnostic systems — including reflectometers, interferometers, ECE radiometers, flux loops, and barometry gages — where the measurement position is defined by a single spatial point rather than an endpoint of a line of sight. Typical values range from approximately \(-2\) m to \(+2\) m for medium-to-large tokamaks, with \(Z = 0\) at the machine midplane. For more precisely defined geometric loci on diagnostic lines of sight, see vertical_coordinate_of_first_point_of_line_of_sight, vertical_coordinate_of_second_point_of_line_of_sight, and vertical_coordinate_of_third_point_of_line_of_sight; for antenna-based diagnostics see vertical_coordinate_of_centroid_of_diagnostic_antenna. The companion major-radius coordinate for the same measurement position is major_radius_of_launching_position for launcher-type diagnostics.

    Sign convention: Positive when the measurement position is above the machine midplane in the \(+Z\) direction of the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame.

    direction_unit_vector¶

    x_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror¶

    X-axis projection of the unit vector that encodes the instantaneous movement direction of the steerable electron cyclotron (EC) launcher mirror in the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame.

    The EC launcher mirror movement direction is fully specified by the unit vector \(\hat{d} = (d_x, d_y, d_z)\), where \(d_x\) is this quantity. Because \(\hat{d}\) is a unit vector by construction, the three Cartesian components satisfy \(d_x^2 + d_y^2 + d_z^2 = 1\), so \(d_x \in [-1, 1]\). The mirror direction vector describes how the reflective surface tilts or rotates to steer the injected high-power microwave beam toward the desired deposition location inside the plasma; the \(\hat{x}\) component captures the projection of that motion onto the machine Cartesian \(x\)-axis. In practice, \(d_x\) is either set by a geometric model of the launcher mechanism or read directly from mirror position encoders, and it enters beam-tracing and ray-tracing codes that compute the electron cyclotron resonance heating (ECRH) or electron cyclotron current drive (ECCD) power deposition profile. Together with the companion components y_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror and z_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror, it reconstructs the full direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror. The orientation frame of the mirror itself is separately described by the local unit-vector triad, whose x-projections include x_component_of_first_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror.

    Sign convention: Positive when the mirror movement direction has a component in the \(+\hat{x}\) direction of the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame.

    y_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror¶

    Y-axis projection of the unit vector encoding the instantaneous movement direction of the steerable electron cyclotron (EC) launcher mirror in the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame.

    The quantity \(d_y = \hat{d} \cdot \hat{y}\) is the \(y\)-component of the mirror direction unit vector \(\hat{d} = (d_x, d_y, d_z)\), which specifies how the EC launcher mirror is currently moving as it steers the injected microwave beam toward its intended deposition location in the plasma. Together with x_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror and z_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror, it completes the Cartesian triplet satisfying the unit-vector constraint \(d_x^2 + d_y^2 + d_z^2 = 1\). The parent quantity direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror encodes the full three-dimensional movement direction; this component isolates the horizontal \(y\)-axis contribution in the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame co-located with the launcher assembly. The component is dimensionless and bounded on \([-1, 1]\); a value of \(\pm 1\) indicates movement purely along \(\pm\hat{y}\), while zero means the mirror moves with no \(y\)-axis component. This quantity is used in beam-steering control systems and ray-tracing codes to compute the beam injection angle and the corresponding deposition location in the plasma, complementing the orientation frame defined by first_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror and second_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror.

    Sign convention: Positive when the mirror movement direction has a component along \(+\hat{y}\) in the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame.

    z_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror¶

    Vertical component of the unit direction vector that encodes the instantaneous movement direction of the steerable EC launcher mirror in the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame, where \(\hat{z}\) is aligned with the machine vertical axis \(Z\).

    The Z-component \(d_z\) of the direction unit vector of the EC launcher mirror specifies how much of the mirror's instantaneous movement direction projects onto the vertical axis \(\hat{z}\) of the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame, where \(\hat{z}\) coincides with the machine vertical direction. Because the parent vector is a unit vector by construction, the three Cartesian components satisfy \(d_x^2 + d_y^2 + d_z^2 = 1\), so \(d_z \in [-1, 1]\). A value of \(d_z = \pm 1\) indicates purely vertical mirror movement, while \(d_z = 0\) means the movement lies entirely in the horizontal plane. This component, together with x_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror and y_component_of_direction_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror, fully resolves the mirror movement direction needed for beam-steering calculations in electron cyclotron heating and current drive systems. The direction vector is typically obtained from launcher geometry specifications or actuator kinematics models and is used in ray-tracing and beam-tracing codes to propagate the injected microwave beam trajectory through the plasma.

    Sign convention: Positive when the mirror movement direction has a component in the \(+\hat{z}\) direction (upward, toward increasing \(Z\)).

    height_of_ion_cyclotron_heating_antenna_strap¶

    Vertical full height of the rectangular cross-section of an ICRH antenna strap conductor, defining its extent in the poloidal (Z) direction.

    The height of an ion cyclotron resonance heating (ICRH) antenna strap is the full vertical extent of its rectangular cross-section, measured along the \(Z\) axis of the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame where \(Z\) is the vertical coordinate. Together with width and thickness, this dimension fully characterizes the cross-sectional geometry of the strap conductor, which governs its current-carrying capacity, RF impedance, and thermal loading during high-power operation. The strap cross-sectional geometry is a fixed engineering parameter specified at design time and verified by metrology; typical heights for tokamak ICRH straps fall in the range of a few centimeters to tens of centimeters depending on the machine size and power requirements. The vertical coordinate of the rectangle center locates the strap in the poloidal plane, while the length describes the strap's extent along its principal conductor axis.

    Sign convention: Positive when the height extends in the direction of increasing \(Z\) (upward from the machine midplane).

    length_of_ion_cyclotron_heating_antenna_strap¶

    length_of_ion_cyclotron_heating_antenna_strap¶

    Length of an ion cyclotron resonance heating (ICRH) antenna strap conductor measured along its longest axis in the oblique cross-sectional geometry, corresponding to the strap's extent in the direction perpendicular to its cross-section plane. This dimension is distinct from the strap width and thickness, which characterize the cross-sectional face.

    Length of ICRH Antenna Strap¶

    This quantity specifies the length of an individual strap conductor belonging to an ion cyclotron resonance heating (ICRH) antenna module, as stored at ic_antennas/antenna/module/strap/geometry/oblique/length in the IMAS data dictionary.

    In the oblique strap geometry, the strap is characterized by three independent linear dimensions: - length: the extent along the strap's longest (longitudinal) axis, i.e., the dimension perpendicular to the oblique cross-sectional face; - width: the cross-sectional dimension in the poloidal or toroidal direction, depending on strap orientation; - height (or thickness): the remaining cross-sectional dimension.

    The length dimension governs the resonant electrical behavior of the strap and is a key parameter in antenna modeling codes (e.g., TOPICA, ANTITER). Typical ICRH strap lengths on medium-to-large tokamaks range from approximately \(0.3\,\mathrm{m}\) to \(1.5\,\mathrm{m}\), consistent with quarter-wave or half-wave resonance conditions at frequencies in the range \(25\text{–}120\,\mathrm{MHz}\).

    The oblique geometry means the strap cross-section is not necessarily aligned with the cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame; the length is always measured along the conductor's own longitudinal axis regardless of its orientation in the machine. When precise directional decomposition is required, companion quantities such as the toroidal component of the strap width should be consulted.

    second_unit_vector¶

    z_component_of_second_unit_vector_of_electron_cyclotron_launcher_mirror¶

    Projection of the second local unit vector of the electron cyclotron launcher mirror orientation frame onto the vertical axis \(\hat{z}\) of the right-handed Cartesian frame, where \(\hat{z}\) coincides with the machine vertical direction \(\hat{Z}\).

    The Z component \(\hat{e}_{2,z}\) is the projection of the second axis of the orthonormal triad \(\{\hat{e}_1, \hat{e}_2, \hat{e}_3\}\) onto the vertical direction \(\hat{z}\), which coincides with \(\hat{Z}\) in the right-handed Cartesian \((\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})\) frame.     momentum_flux¶

    parallel_component_of_ion_state_momentum_flux¶

    Parallel component of the momentum flux tensor for a specific ion charge state, obtained by projecting the full momentum flux onto the local magnetic field direction. This quantity captures the field-aligned transport of ion momentum per unit area for that charge state in the edge plasma.

    The parallel component of the momentum flux tensor for a specific ion charge state is defined as the projection of the momentum flux density tensor \(\mathbf{\Pi}_s\) of ion charge state \(s\) onto the unit vector \(\hat{b} = \mathbf{B}/|\mathbf{B}|\) along the local magnetic field:

    \[\Pi_{\parallel,s} = \hat{b} \cdot \mathbf{\Pi}_s \cdot \hat{b} = \rho_s v_{\parallel,s}^2 + p_{\parallel,s}\]

    where \(\rho_s\) (kg m\(^{-3}\)) is the mass density of charge state \(s\), \(v_{\parallel,s}\) (m s\(^{-1}\)) is the parallel fluid velocity of that charge state, and \(p_{\parallel,s}\) (Pa = kg m\(^{-1}\) s\(^{-2}\)) is the parallel component of the (potentially anisotropic) pressure tensor for that charge state. In a magnetized edge plasma, the pressure tensor is generally anisotropic, so \(p_{\parallel,s}\) differs from the perpendicular pressure \(p_{\perp,s}\); using the isotropic scalar pressure \(p_s\) is only valid in the limit of weak anisotropy.

    Sign convention: Positive when the net momentum flux is directed in the direction of the local magnetic field unit vector \(\hat{b}\) (i.e., in the direction of increasing field-line arc length along \(\hat{b}\)).

    This quantity is stored as a GGD (Generalized Grid Description) scalar field at the locations defined by the GGD grid subset, with one value per grid element. The corresponding data dictionary path is edge_transport/model/ggd/ion/state/momentum/flux_parallel/values in the edge_transport IDS.

    Typical magnitude ranges in edge plasmas vary widely with plasma conditions: from approximately \(10^{-2}\) kg m\(^{-1}\) s\(^{-2}\) in cold, low-density divertor detachment conditions up to \(\sim 10^1\) kg m\(^{-1}\) s\(^{-2}\) in hot attached scrape-off-layer plasmas. The ion charge state index distinguishes contributions from different ionization states of a given ion species, enabling detailed multi-fluid transport modeling.

    The parallel momentum flux is a key quantity in edge transport models, governing the parallel flow of ions along field lines toward divertor targets and playing a central role in momentum balance equations that determine the plasma flow profile in the scrape-off layer.

    Field-aligned component of the momentum flux carried by neutral particles in the edge plasma, summed over all neutral species.     maximum_magnetic_field_strength_on_flux_surface¶

    Maximum of the total magnetic field modulus |B| taken over all points on each flux surface, yielding a positive-definite scalar for each surface label. This quantity enters neoclassical transport theory and determines the threshold pitch angle for magnetically trapped particles.

    Maximum Magnetic Field Strength on Flux Surface¶

    For a given flux surface labeled by a normalized flux coordinate (e.g., \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}} = \sqrt{\Phi/\Phi_{\mathrm{boundary}}}\), where \(\Phi\) is the toroidal magnetic flux), this quantity is defined as

    \[B_{\max}(\rho) = \sup_{\theta,\phi}\, |\mathbf{B}(R(\rho,\theta,\phi),\,\phi,\,Z(\rho,\theta,\phi))|,\]

    where the supremum is taken over all poloidal angles \(\theta\) and toroidal angles \(\phi\) on the surface, and \(|\mathbf{B}|\) is evaluated in the right-handed cylindrical \((R,\phi,Z)\) frame. The result is always strictly positive regardless of the sign convention applied to individual magnetic field components.

    Physical context. In an axisymmetric tokamak the toroidal dependence drops out and the maximum is attained at the high-field side (small \(R\)), where the dominant toroidal field component \(B_\phi \sim B_0 R_0 / R\) is largest. Typical values in conventional tokamaks range from roughly 3 T on axis to 6–10 T at the high-field side for high-field devices.

    \(B_{\max}\) together with \(B_{\min}\) (the minimum on the same surface) determines the local mirror ratio \(R_{\mathrm{mirror}} = B_{\max}/B_{\min}\), which governs the fraction of magnetically trapped particles. A particle with velocity-space pitch angle \(\lambda = v_\perp^2 / (v^2 B)\) satisfies the trapping condition when \(\lambda > 1/B_{\max}\), i.e., when its magnetic moment prevents it from passing through the field maximum. This in turn controls neoclassical transport coefficients such as the bootstrap current and thermal conductivity.

A closely related path equilibrium/time_slice/profiles_1d/b_max stores Maximum(B) on the flux surface and may differ in treatment of field-direction sign. Both paths are indexed along the same 1D flux-surface grid as other profiles_1d quantities.

    Sign convention: Positive definite; \(B_{\max}\) is the modulus and carries no sign.

    collisional_power_density_to_thermal_electron¶

    trapped_fast_particle_collisional_power_density_to_thermal_electron¶

    Volumetric power density deposited into the thermal electron population via Coulomb collisions with trapped fast (non-thermal) particles. This is the locally evaluated energy exchange rate per unit volume attributable exclusively to the trapped-orbit subset of the fast-particle distribution, and is positive when net energy flows from fast particles to thermal electrons.

    Trapped Fast-Particle Collisional Power Density to Thermal Electrons¶

    Physical definition¶

    This quantity gives the local volumetric power density \(Q_{\mathrm{trap},e}^{\mathrm{coll}}\) (units: \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\)) transferred from the trapped-orbit subset of the fast-particle distribution to the thermal electron population through Coulomb collisions (electron drag and pitch-angle scattering). It is defined as

    \[Q_{\mathrm{trap},e}^{\mathrm{coll}}(\mathbf{x}) = \int_{\mathrm{trapped}} u_{se}(v)\, E_k\, f_f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})\, d^3v,\]

    where the integration is restricted to trapped-orbit phase-space (i.e., particles whose guiding-center bounce trajectory does not complete a full toroidal transit), $ u_{se}(v)$ is the velocity-dependent slowing-down frequency on electrons, \(E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}m_f v^2\) is the fast-particle kinetic energy, and \(f_f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})\) is the fast-particle distribution function in units of \(\mathrm{m^{-6}\,s^3}\) (number of particles per unit phase-space volume). The integral therefore carries units of \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) — power per unit real-space volume, evaluated at position \(\mathbf{x}\) in the IMAS cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame.

    Orbit-class partitioning¶

    The trapped subset is distinguished from the passing subsets (co-passing and counter-passing) by the trapping condition on the pitch-angle variable \(\xi = v_\parallel / v\): a particle at position \(\mathbf{x}\) is classified as trapped when \(|\xi| < \xi_{\mathrm{trap}}(\mathbf{x})\), where \(\xi_{\mathrm{trap}} = \sqrt{1 - B(\mathbf{x})/B_{\mathrm{max}}}\) and \(B_{\mathrm{max}}\) is the maximum magnetic field along the flux surface. Summing the trapped, co-passing, and counter-passing contributions exactly reconstructs the total collisional power density to thermal electrons: \(Q_{\mathrm{total},e}^{\mathrm{coll}} = Q_{\mathrm{trap},e}^{\mathrm{coll}} + Q_{\mathrm{co},e}^{\mathrm{coll}} + Q_{\mathrm{ctr},e}^{\mathrm{coll}}\).

    Sign convention¶

    Sign convention: Positive when net energy flows from the trapped fast-particle population into the thermal electrons (heating). A negative value would indicate anomalous back-transfer, which is unphysical under classical Coulomb collisions but may appear as a numerical artifact.

    Volumetric power density transferred from co-passing fast particles to bulk thermal ions via Coulomb collisions. One of three orbit-class contributions (co-passing, counter-passing, trapped) to the total fast-particle-to-ion collisional heating source.

    This quantity is the collisional power density delivered to the bulk thermal ion population by the co-passing orbit-class subset of the fast-particle distribution. It is one of three orbit-class components — co-passing, counter-passing, and trapped — that together constitute the total fast-particle-to-thermal-ion heating source term in the ion energy balance equation.

    Physical definition: the power per unit volume transferred from co-passing fast particles to thermal ions through Coulomb friction and diffusion. The integral is restricted to the co-passing velocity-space domain (\(v_\parallel > 0\), \(|v_\parallel/v| > \lambda_{\mathrm{T/P}}\), where \(\lambda_{\mathrm{T/P}}\) is the trapped–passing boundary).

    Typical values in neutral-beam-heated tokamak plasmas range from \(10^3\) to \(10^6\) W m\(^{-3}\), depending on fast-ion density, beam energy, and plasma temperature.

    fast_particle_collisional_power_density_to_thermal_ion¶

    Volumetric power density transferred from the total fast (suprathermal) particle population to bulk thermal ions via Coulomb collisions, summed over all orbit classes (co-passing, counter-passing, and trapped).

    This quantity gives the net collisional energy exchange rate per unit volume from the entire fast-particle distribution — encompassing co-passing, counter-passing, and trapped orbit classes — to the bulk thermal ion population. It appears as a source term \(S_{fi}\) in the ion energy balance equation

    \[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{3}{2} n_i T_i\right) + abla \cdot \mathbf{q}_i = S_{fi} + \ldots\]

    where \(n_i\) is the thermal ion density, \(T_i\) is the thermal ion temperature, and \(\mathbf{q}_i\) is the ion heat flux. The exchange rate arises from Coulomb drag: energetic particles slow down on the background plasma, depositing power preferentially to ions when the fast-particle velocity \(v_f\) is below the critical slowing-down velocity \(v_c \approx (3\sqrt{\pi}/4)^{1/3}(m_i/m_e)^{1/6}v_{\mathrm{th},e}\), where \(m_i\) and \(m_e\) are the ion and electron masses and \(v_{\mathrm{th},e}\) is the electron thermal velocity.

    In neutral-beam-injected or ICRH-heated plasmas, this power density governs the fraction of auxiliary heating that goes to the ions rather than electrons; typical values range from \(10^5\) to \(10^8\) W m\(^{-3}\) in the core of medium-to-large tokamaks. It is computed from Fokker–Planck or Monte Carlo distribution-function codes applied to the fast-particle distribution function \(f(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v})\).

    Orbit-class-resolved contributions are captured by co-passing fast particle collisional power density to thermal ion and counter-passing fast particle collisional power density to thermal ion, while trapped fast particle collisional power density transferred to thermal ions covers the trapped sub-population. The analogous transfer to the electron channel is described by fast particle collisional power density due to Coulomb collisions with electrons. For the charge-state-resolved version, see fast particle collisional power density to thermal ion state due to Coulomb collisions.

    trapped_fast_particle_collisional_power_density_to_thermal_ion¶

    Collisional power density transferred from trapped fast particles to thermal ions via Coulomb collisions, giving the local volumetric heating rate of the bulk ion fluid attributable to the trapped suprathermal population. Positive values indicate net energy deposition into the thermal ions.

    Trapped Fast-Particle Collisional Power Density to Thermal Ions¶

    This quantity is the local volumetric rate at which energy is transferred from the trapped suprathermal (fast) particle population to the thermal ion fluid through Coulomb collisions. It is defined as the velocity-space moment of the collisional energy-exchange operator \(C[f_\mathrm{fast}]\) weighted by the particle kinetic energy \(\tfrac{1}{2}m v^2\):

    \[p_\mathrm{coll} = \int \frac{1}{2}m v^2\, C[f_\mathrm{fast}, f_\mathrm{ion}]\, d^3v \quad [\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}]\]

    where \(f_\mathrm{fast}\) is the trapped fast-particle distribution function, \(f_\mathrm{ion}\) is the thermal ion distribution function, and the integral is performed over the trapped portion of velocity space. The result is a scalar field resolved locally in the plasma volume (or along a flux-surface-averaged profile, depending on the IDS context), stored with units W m\(^{-3}\).

    In the slowing-down picture, energy is transferred from suprathermal particles to the thermal ions at a rate proportional to the ion-drag friction force \(F_\mathrm{drag}^\mathrm{ion}(v)\) integrated over the fast-particle velocity distribution. The fraction of power deposited on ions (as opposed to electrons) depends strongly on the fast-particle injection energy relative to the critical energy \(E_c = \tfrac{1}{2}m_f v_c^2\), where \(v_c\) is the critical velocity separating ion-dominated and electron-dominated slowing-down regimes. For \(E_\mathrm{fast} \gg E_c\), the majority of fast-particle energy flows to electrons; for \(E_\mathrm{fast} \ll E_c\), energy flows predominantly to ions.

    Typical values in reactor-relevant scenarios range from \(10^4\) to \(10^7\) W m\(^{-3}\), depending on plasma density, fast-particle source strength (e.g., neutral beam or fusion alpha), and radial location.

    fast_particle_density¶

    Number density of fast (suprathermal) particles on a given flux surface, summed over all orbit classes and velocity-space pitch angles, representing the total non-thermal contribution to the local particle inventory.

    The fast-particle number density \(n_\mathrm{fast}\) is defined as the zeroth velocity-space moment of the suprathermal distribution function \(f_\mathrm{fast}(\mathbf{v})\), integrated over all velocities above the suprathermal threshold \(v_\mathrm{th,cut}\) and over all pitch angles \(\xi = v_\parallel/v\):

    \[n_\mathrm{fast} = \int_{v > v_\mathrm{th,cut}} \int_{-1}^{1} f_\mathrm{fast}(v, \xi)\, 2\pi v^2\, d\xi\, dv\]

    The quantity reported on a flux surface is the flux-surface average

    \[\langle n_\mathrm{fast} \rangle = \frac{\oint n_\mathrm{fast}\, dA / | abla \psi|}{\oint dA / | abla \psi|}\]

    where the surface integral is taken at constant normalized poloidal flux \(\psi\).

    The suprathermal threshold \(v_\mathrm{th,cut}\) is typically defined as a multiple of the local thermal speed \(v_\mathrm{th} = \sqrt{2 k_B T / m}\), often taken as \(v_\mathrm{th,cut} \approx 2\text{--}3\, v_\mathrm{th}\), but its precise definition is model- and code-dependent. When the fast-particle population is anisotropic (e.g., beam-injected ions or ICRH-heated minority species), the threshold should be understood as applying to the total speed \(v = |\mathbf{v}|\) and includes contributions from all pitch angles; no assumption of isotropy is made.

    Typical values range from \(\sim 10^{16}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}\) for weakly heated discharges to \(\sim 10^{18}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}\) in strongly NBI- or ICRH-driven plasmas. Fast particles from fusion-born alpha particles contribute at the upper end of this range in burning plasmas.

It complements co-passing fast particle density and counter-passing fast particle density, which together with this quantity partition fast_particle_density by orbit class. The corresponding all-energy (thermal plus non-thermal) trapped population is described by trapped_particle_density.

    trapped_particle_density¶

    Total number density of particles — thermal plus non-thermal — whose parallel velocity is small enough that the magnetic mirror force confines them to a bounded poloidal arc rather than allowing full poloidal transit. Equivalently, these are particles with pitch angle exceeding the local trapping criterion defined by the magnetic field variation on a flux surface.

    Trapped Particle Density¶

    The trapped particle density \(n_\mathrm{trap}\) is the total number of trapped-orbit particles (thermal and non-thermal) per unit volume on a given flux surface. A particle is classified as trapped when its pitch angle \(\lambda = v_\parallel / v\) satisfies the local mirror trapping condition

    \[|v_\parallel| < v \sqrt{1 - B_\mathrm{min}/B}\]

    where \(B\) is the local magnetic field magnitude and \(B_\mathrm{min}\) is the minimum field on the same flux surface. Such particles undergo bounce oscillations between mirror points and do not complete full poloidal circuits.

    The quantity is resolved on flux surfaces and stored as a function of the minor-radial coordinate (typically \(\rho_\mathrm{tor,norm}\), the normalized toroidal flux radius) and optionally on a two-dimensional \((R, Z)\) poloidal grid in the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame where \(R\) is the major radius, \(\phi\) is the toroidal angle increasing counter-clockwise when viewed from above, and \(Z\) is the vertical height. It is NOT a bounce-averaged quantity; it represents the local particle density integrated over the trapped portion of velocity space at each spatial point.

    The orbit-class partition identity reads

    \[n_\mathrm{total} = n_\mathrm{trap} + n_\mathrm{co} + n_\mathrm{counter}\]

    where \(n_\mathrm{co}\) and \(n_\mathrm{counter}\) are the co-passing and counter-passing densities respectively.

    Typical values: In a deuterium plasma with \(n_e \sim 10^{19}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}\) and inverse aspect ratio \(\epsilon = r/R \sim 0.1\)–\(0.3\), the trapped fraction \(f_t \approx 1.46\sqrt{\epsilon} - 0.46\epsilon\) implies \(n_\mathrm{trap} \sim (0.3\)–\(0.6)\,n_\mathrm{total}\).

    \[v_{E\times B,Z} = \frac{(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\hat{Z}}{|B|^2} = \frac{E_R B_\phi - E_\phi B_R}{|B|^2}\]

    where \(E_R\), \(E_\phi\) are the radial and toroidal components of the electric field and \(B_R\), \(B_\phi\) are the corresponding magnetic field components in the \((R, \phi, Z)\) basis.

    This drift acts on all charged species (ions and electrons) but does not apply to neutral particles, which are unaffected by electromagnetic forces. Neutral velocity components along \(Z\) are separately described by convective or pressure-driven transport.

    radial_component_of_electric_field¶

    Component of the plasma electric field along the major-radius direction R in the right-handed cylindrical (R, phi, Z) frame. It is the projection E_R = E · R-hat, obtained from the negative radial gradient of the electrostatic potential. This component drives cross-field drifts and is central to radial force balance in magnetized plasmas.

    The radial electric field \(E_R\) is the component of the total electric field \(\mathbf{E}\) along the major-radius unit vector \(\hat{R}\) in the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame, where \(R\) is the major radius measured from the machine symmetry axis and \(Z\) is the vertical height.

    It is defined by the projection

    \[E_R = \mathbf{E} \cdot \hat{R} = -\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial R},\]

    where \(\Phi\) is the electrostatic potential. This quantity is stored in the IMAS IDS at core_profiles/profiles_1d/e_field/radial and is typically provided as a function of a radial flux-surface coordinate such as \(\rho_\mathrm{tor,norm}\) (normalized toroidal flux radius).

    Note that in a general shaped equilibrium, the direction \(\hat{R}\) is not identical to the direction perpendicular to a flux surface; the flux-surface-normal direction acquires contributions from both \(\hat{R}\) and \(\hat{Z}\). The quantity \(E_R\) therefore represents the laboratory-frame cylindrical projection and should not be equated directly with the flux-surface-perpendicular electric field \(E_\perp\) except in the limit of circular, concentric flux surfaces.

    Radial force balance. For a given ion species \(s\) with charge \(q_s\), the radial force-balance relation reads

    \[q_s E_R = \frac{1}{n_s}\frac{\partial p_s}{\partial R} - v_{\phi,s} B_Z + v_{Z,s} B_\phi,\]

    where \(n_s\) is the species density, \(p_s\) is the pressure, and \(v_{\phi,s}\), \(v_{Z,s}\) are the toroidal and vertical flow components respectively, with \(B_\phi\) and \(B_Z\) the corresponding magnetic-field components.

    \(E\times B\) rotation. The \(E\times B\) drift velocity in the poloidal plane, which drives toroidal plasma rotation, involves a combination of field components and is geometry-dependent; it cannot in general be reduced to \(E_R / B\) alone. A correct expression for the \(E\times B\) toroidal angular frequency requires the full poloidal-field geometry.

    Typical values. In tokamak core plasmas, \(|E_R|\) is typically in the range \(10^2\)–\(10^4\) V m\(^{-1}\). In the edge and pedestal region, sharp \(E_R\) wells of order \(10^4\)–\(10^5\) V m\(^{-1}\) are observed, associated with the L–H transition and suppression of turbulence.

    momentum_convection_velocity_due_to_diamagnetic_drift¶

    Effective scalar radial convective velocity in the momentum transport equation arising from the diamagnetic drift of a charged species. Represents the net radial advection speed of momentum density across flux surfaces due to pressure-gradient-driven cross-field drifts.

    In fluid descriptions of magnetized plasma transport, the momentum flux across flux surfaces contains a convective contribution proportional to a radial convective velocity \(v_\mathrm{dia}\). This quantity is defined as the effective radial projection of the diamagnetic drift that enters the radial momentum transport equation:

    \[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(m_s n_s u_{s,\parallel}) + \frac{1}{V'} \frac{\partial}{\partial \rho} \left[ V' \left( -D \frac{\partial (m_s n_s u_{s,\parallel})}{\partial \rho} + v_\mathrm{dia} \, m_s n_s u_{s,\parallel} \right) \right] = \ldots\]

    where \(\rho\) is a radial flux-surface label (e.g., \(\rho_\mathrm{tor,norm}\)), \(V'= \partial V/\partial\rho\) is the flux-surface volume derivative, \(m_s\) is the species mass, \(n_s\) the density, \(u_{s,\parallel}\) the parallel flow velocity, and \(D\) is the diffusion coefficient.

    The stored scalar \(v_\mathrm{dia}\) (in m/s) is the effective radial convective speed extracted from the full vector diamagnetic drift. The underlying vector diamagnetic drift velocity for a charged species with charge \(q_s\) is:

    \[\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{dia},s} = \frac{- abla p_s \times \mathbf{B}}{q_s n_s B^2}\]

    where \(p_s\) is the species pressure and \(\mathbf{B}\) is the magnetic field. The scalar \(v_\mathrm{dia}\) stored in the transport IDS is the flux-surface-averaged radial component of this drift projected onto the minor-radius direction \(\hat{\rho}\) (outward from the magnetic axis in the poloidal plane of the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame):

    \[v_\mathrm{dia} = \langle \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{dia},s} \cdot \hat{\rho} \rangle\]

    where the angle brackets denote the flux-surface average. The precise numerical value depends on geometry and the transport model performing the flux-surface averaging.

    Typical magnitudes for ion species in tokamak plasmas are \(10^1\)–\(10^4\) m/s, with larger values in steep-gradient regions such as the edge pedestal.

    Note: The linked DD path plasma_transport/model/profiles_1d/neutral/state/momentum/v/diamagnetic stores this quantity for neutral states. In standard physics, neutral particles do not experience the magnetic diamagnetic drift. When a value appears under a neutral species path, it represents an effective convective velocity assigned by the transport model's fluid closure (e.g., from charge-exchange coupling or an effective neutral fluid approximation), not a direct magnetic drift of the neutrals themselves.

    The quantity is evaluated at each radial location on the transport grid associated with the plasma transport IDS.

    Sign convention: Positive when the effective radial convective velocity is directed outward, away from the magnetic axis along the direction of increasing minor radius.

    momentum_flow_damping_rate¶

    parallel_component_of_ion_momentum_flow_damping_rate¶

    Rate at which the component of ion fluid momentum flow along the local magnetic field direction is dissipated by collisional friction or neoclassical viscosity in the plasma transport model.

    The parallel component of the ion momentum flow damping rate, $ u_{\parallel,\rm damp}$, is the scalar coefficient appearing in the parallel projection of the ion momentum sink term:

    \[\left(\frac{\partial (m_i n_i u_{i\parallel})}{\partial t}\right)_{\rm damp} = - u_{\parallel,\rm damp}\, m_i n_i u_{i\parallel}\]

    where \(m_i\) is the ion mass, \(n_i\) is the ion number density, and \(u_{i\parallel} = \mathbf{u}_i \cdot \hat{b}\) is the ion fluid velocity projected onto the unit vector \(\hat{b}\) along the magnetic field. The parallel direction is special in magnetized plasmas because parallel momentum transport is governed predominantly by ion–ion collisions and parallel viscosity, whereas perpendicular components are additionally regulated by neoclassical poloidal-flow damping (Stringer spin-up and neoclassical flow damping). Physically, $ u_{\parallel,\rm damp}$ encapsulates contributions from ion–ion collisional friction, parallel viscous damping, and any neoclassical effects projected onto the field-aligned direction. Typical values in the tokamak core range from \(10^2\) to \(10^5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) depending on collisionality and plasma density; in the high-collisionality (Pfirsch–Schlüter) regime the rate approaches the ion–ion collision frequency $ u_{ii}$. This quantity is a member of the directional decomposition family of ion_momentum_flow_damping_rate, which also includes the radial_component_of_ion_momentum_flow_damping_rate, poloidal_component_of_ion_momentum_flow_damping_rate, and toroidal_component_of_ion_momentum_flow_damping_rate. In transport codes it is computed from neoclassical models (e.g. NCLASS, NEO) or from collisional closure schemes applied along flux surfaces.

    Sign convention: Positive when the damping acts to reduce the parallel ion momentum, i.e. when \(u_{i\parallel}\) and the resulting momentum sink have the same sign as \(u_{i\parallel}\) itself (damping coefficient positive definite).

    derivative_of_electron_density_with_respect_to_normalized_toroidal_flux_coordinate¶

    The quantity \(\partial n_e / \partial \rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}}\) gives the radial gradient of the total electron number density — including both thermal and non-thermal (fast) contributions — with respect to the normalized toroidal flux coordinate \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}} \in [0, 1]\), where \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}} = 0\) at the magnetic axis and \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}} = 1\) at the last closed flux surface. Because \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}}\) is dimensionless, the derivative carries the same dimensions as density itself. This gradient appears directly in the electron particle transport equation,

    \[\frac{\partial n_e}{\partial t} + abla \cdot \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e = S_e,\]

    where \(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\) is the electron particle flux and \(S_e\) is the source term; the Fickian diffusive flux is proportional to this gradient via the electron particle diffusion coefficient. In transport solver codes, the derivative is evaluated numerically on the 1-D radial grid in \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}}\) from density profiles obtained via Thomson scattering, interferometry, or reflectometry. Typical core gradients in H-mode tokamaks range from \(-10^{20}\) to \(-10^{19}\) m\(^{-3}\) per unit \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}}\), with steeper pedestals approaching \(-10^{21}\) m\(^{-3}\).     Second derivative of the fast (suprathermal) electron number density with respect to the normalized toroidal flux coordinate \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}}\), characterizing the radial curvature of the non-thermal electron density profile across nested flux surfaces.

    The fast electron population consists of suprathermal electrons whose kinetic energy substantially exceeds the bulk thermal energy, arising from sources such as runaway electron generation, radio-frequency heating, or beam injection. This quantity is the second derivative \(\partial^2 n_{\mathrm{fe}} / \partial \rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}}^2\), where \(n_{\mathrm{fe}}\) is the fast electron number density and \(\rho_{\mathrm{tor,norm}} = \sqrt{\Phi_{\mathrm{tor}} / \Phi_{\mathrm{tor,edge}}}\) is the normalized toroidal flux coordinate (dimensionless, ranging from 0 at the magnetic axis to 1 at the plasma boundary). The second derivative quantifies the local concavity of the fast electron density profile and appears as a higher-order term in transport solver discretizations, entering the radial diffusion operator for the fast electron continuity equation alongside the first derivative captured by first derivative of fast electron density. Typical fast electron densities in auxiliary-heated tokamak plasmas range from \(10^{16}\) to \(10^{18}\) m\(^{-3}\); the magnitude of the second derivative depends sensitively on source localization and transport properties. This quantity is not directly measured but is computed numerically from the radial fast electron density profile during transport solver iterations.

    co_passing_particle_density¶

    Total particle number density (thermal plus non-thermal) of the co-passing population, comprising particles whose guiding-center orbits circulate toroidally in the same direction as the net plasma current. Distinct from the trapped and counter-passing populations that together make up the total particle density.

    The co-passing particle density \(n_\mathrm{co}\) is the number density of particles whose guiding-center motion executes complete toroidal transits in the co-current direction, i.e., in the direction of increasing toroidal angle \(\phi\) when the plasma current \(I_p\) is positive (flowing in the \(+\hat{\phi}\) direction of the right-handed cylindrical \((R, \phi, Z)\) frame). Classification as co-passing versus counter-passing depends on the sign of the parallel velocity projected onto the local magnetic field direction weighted by the direction of the net toroidal current, and not merely on the sign of the local \(v_\parallel\): a particle is co-passing if its orbit-averaged toroidal angular velocity shares the sign of \(I_p\).

    The density is computed as the zeroth moment of the co-passing part of the distribution function \(f_\mathrm{co}(\mathbf{r}, v_\parallel, v_\perp)\) over velocity space:

    \[n_\mathrm{co}(R,Z) = \int_{v_\parallel / v > v_{\parallel,\mathrm{trap}}(R,Z)} f_\mathrm{co}\, 2\pi v_\perp\, dv_\perp\, dv_\parallel\]

    where the lower limit on \(v_\parallel\) is set by the trapping condition at the local flux surface. Both thermal and non-thermal (suprathermal, beam-injected, and fast) particles contribute.

    Typical values in a tokamak core range from \(10^{17}\) to \(10^{20}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}\) depending on species, plasma current, and heating scenario. In nearly axisymmetric plasmas with a symmetric distribution function the co- and counter-passing densities are nearly equal; asymmetric heating (e.g., co-directed neutral beam injection) or current drive creates a net imbalance.

    This quantity is stored in the IMAS distributions IDS at the path distributions/distribution/profiles_2d/co_passing/density (unit: m\(^{-3}\)), where the co_passing node distinguishes this population from the counter_passing and trapped sub-populations. The total particle density is the sum of co-passing, counter-passing, and trapped densities integrated over the full distribution.

    counter_passing_particle_density¶

    Total particle number density of the counter-passing population (thermal plus non-thermal), comprising particles whose guiding-center parallel velocity is directed opposite to the plasma current along the magnetic field. The orbit-class boundary is defined by the local pitch angle at the trapping-passing boundary, typically |v_parallel/v| > lambda_crit, and is model-dependent.

    The counter-passing particle density \(n_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) is the number density of all particles (thermal and non-thermal combined) whose guiding-center parallel velocity \(v_\|\) is directed antiparallel to the net plasma current \(I_p\) along the magnetic-field line.

    More precisely, the passing–trapping boundary is set at the critical pitch-angle cosine \(\lambda_c = v_{\|}/v\) for which the particle orbit just closes without mirroring; particles with \(|\lambda| > \lambda_c\) are passing, and among those the counter-passing subset satisfies \(\mathrm{sgn}(v_\|) = -\mathrm{sgn}(B_\phi \cdot I_p / |B_\phi \cdot I_p|)\), i.e. their parallel velocity opposes the field-aligned component of the current. The precise value of \(\lambda_c\) depends on the magnetic geometry and the distribution-function model used; it is not a universal constant.

    The total particle density partitions as

    \[n = n_{\mathrm{co}} + n_{\mathrm{ctr}} + n_{\mathrm{trapped}},\]

    where \(n_{\mathrm{co}}\) is the co-passing density, \(n_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) is this quantity, and \(n_{\mathrm{trapped}}\) is the trapped-particle density. This bookkeeping identity holds by construction for any distribution-function discretization that assigns each marker to exactly one orbit class.

    Typical values range from \(10^{17}\) m\(^{-3}\) (low-density auxiliary-heated plasmas) to \(\sim 10^{20}\) m\(^{-3}\) (high-density core). Counter-passing fractions relative to \(n\) depend on collisionality regime and heating geometry; in a banana-regime tokamak at moderate aspect ratio the trapped fraction is \(\sim \epsilon^{1/2}\) (\(\epsilon = r/R\) local inverse aspect ratio), so \(n_{\mathrm{ctr}} \approx n_{\mathrm{co}} \approx (1 - \epsilon^{1/2}) n / 2\) under isotropic conditions.

    pressure¶

    fast_ion_pressure¶

    Anisotropic kinetic pressure of the fast (non-thermal) ion population, capturing the full pressure tensor arising from non-Maxwellian velocity distributions of energetic ions in a magnetized plasma.

    Fast ions — alpha particles, neutral-beam-injected ions, and ICRH-accelerated ions — develop strongly anisotropic velocity distributions that cannot be characterized by a single scalar pressure. The fast-ion pressure tensor is most naturally decomposed along and perpendicular to the local magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}\), yielding two independent scalar components: the parallel pressure \(p_{\parallel,f} = m_f \int v_{\parallel}^2 f_f \, d^3v\) and the perpendicular pressure \(p_{\perp,f} = \frac{m_f}{2} \int v_{\perp}^2 f_f \, d^3v\), where \(m_f\) is the fast-ion mass and \(f_f\) is the fast-ion distribution function. The isotropic (trace) contribution to the total pressure balance is \(p_f = (p_{\parallel,f} + 2\,p_{\perp,f})/3\). In ITER-class deuterium–tritium plasmas, fast-ion pressures can reach \(10^4\)–\(10^5\) Pa in the core, representing a significant fraction of the total MHD pressure and thus affecting equilibrium reconstruction and stability analysis. The distribution function is computed from Fokker–Planck or Monte Carlo codes (e.g., using distributions of particle populations), and validated against fast-ion diagnostics such as neutral-particle analyzers and collective Thomson scattering. The directional components are accessed individually as parallel_component_of_fast_ion_pressure and perpendicular_component_of_fast_ion_pressure; the analogous quantity for fast neutrals arising from charge-exchange reactions is fast_neutral_pressure.

    Sign convention: Positive when the fast-ion kinetic energy density is positive, which is always the case for a physical distribution function.
